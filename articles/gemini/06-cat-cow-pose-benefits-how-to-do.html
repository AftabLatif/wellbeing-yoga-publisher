Few yoga movements are as welcoming and useful as Cat-Cow. It is a gentle two-part flow that moves your spine smoothly back and forth in sync with your breath. Whether you are waking up, warming up, or loosening a stiff back after sitting, it is the easiest way to bring healthy movement back into your spine. It is already included as a warm-up in the 10-minute morning yoga routine and the [LINK: Article #4].

What Is Cat-Cow Pose?

On hands and knees (Tabletop): Cow Pose (Bitilasana) — lowering your belly, lifting your chest and tailbone, and softly gazing upward to open the front of your body and gently extend your spine. Cat Pose (Marjaryasana) — rounding your spine to the ceiling, drawing your belly inward, and tucking your chin to your chest to stretch the back of your body. The rhythmic movement creates a wave-like motion along your entire spine, from tailbone to neck.

Key Benefits

  • Improves Spinal Mobility & Flexibility — gently flexes and extends your spine, reducing stiffness in your neck, mid-back and lower back.
  • Relieves Everyday Back & Neck Tension — encourages healthy fluid circulation between your spinal joints and eases tightness from prolonged sitting and hunching.
  • Connects Breath With Movement — pairing your inhale and exhale with each posture shift slows your pace and brings your focus back.
  • Beginner-Friendly & Low Impact — minimal equipment needed, builds body awareness, and is easily adjusted.

How to Do It Step by Step — Tabletop

Set Foundation

Wrists under shoulders, knees under hips and hip-width apart. Spread your fingers wide and press evenly through your palms and knuckles.

Neutral Spine First

Keep your back flat like a table, engage your abdominals lightly, look down between your hands, and keep your neck long.

Inhale Into Cow

Breathe in slowly through your nose, let your belly soften to the floor, lift your chest forward and slightly up, and tilt your sit bones up. Keep your gaze soft and forward, and avoid cranking your neck far back.

Exhale Into Cat

Breathe out smoothly, press your hands into the floor, round your upper and middle back to the ceiling, draw your navel inward, and let your head drop softly, without forcing your chin into your chest.

Flow 6–10 Breaths

Continue slowly. Let your breath guide your pace, and do not rush.

Common Mistakes and Modifications

  • Rushing or Snapping the Back — it is a smooth, mindful stretch. Slow down if the movement feels jerky.
  • Over-Arching the Lower Back — lengthen and distribute the motion evenly along your whole spine, not dumping it into your lower back.
  • Wrist Discomfort — place your hands slightly forward of your shoulders, put a folded towel under your palms, or try the seated version with your hands on your knees (as in the [LINK: Article #2]).
  • Knee Sensitivity — place a folded blanket or cushion under your knees.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many rounds should beginners do? 5–8 slow rounds (about 1–2 minutes) is plenty. Gradually build to 10–12 rounds if it feels comfortable.

Morning or evening? Both — in the morning it wakes a stiff spine, and in the evening it releases tension before the [LINK: Article #3].

Can I do Cat-Cow if my lower back hurts? Gentle, pain-free Cat-Cow is often helpful for mild stiffness. Keep your range comfortable and stop if you feel sharp or pinching pain.
