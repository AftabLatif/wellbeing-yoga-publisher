Whenever a yoga class feels intense — or your mind and back need a quiet pause — Child's Pose (Balasana) is the go-to resting position. Named from the Sanskrit Bala ("child") + Asana ("pose"), this gentle inward-facing posture gives your body permission to soften, release effort, and catch its breath. Even on its own, a few minutes can soothe a tight back, quiet mental noise, and reset you during a busy day. It is also used as a gentle reset in the 10-minute morning yoga routine and the [LINK: Article #4].

Key Benefits

  • Gently Stretches Back, Hips and Thighs — folding forward lengthens the muscles along your spine while softly opening your hips, outer thighs and ankles.
  • Relieves Everyday Tension in Lower Back and Shoulders — your torso draping forward takes weight off your lower spine, and tight shoulders drop away from your ears.
  • Promotes Mental Calm and Stress Relief — tucking inward and breathing slowly into the back of your ribcage encourages a calm, grounded state, especially alongside the [LINK: Article #5].
  • Offers a Safe "Pause Button" Anytime — unlike poses that demand balance or strength, Child's Pose lets you rest and regroup whenever you feel fatigued.

How to Do It Step by Step

Use a mat, carpet or folded blanket so your knees and shins are comfortable.

Start on Hands and Knees

Come to Tabletop, wrists under shoulders and knees under hips. (For alignment, see the [LINK: Article #6].)

Position Your Knees and Big Toes

Bring your big toes together behind you. For a softer, wider hip opening and extra room for your belly and chest, open your knees wider toward the edges of your mat — or keep your knees closer together for a deeper stretch along the back of your spine.

Sit Your Hips Back

Slowly lower your hips back toward your heels. They do not need to touch your heels completely — stop wherever you feel comfortable and steady.

Extend Your Arms and Rest Your Forehead

Walk your hands forward, palms down, and rest your forehead gently on the mat. Alternatively, bring your arms alongside your body, palms up, for a more relaxed shoulder release.

Breathe Slowly for 5–10 Breaths

Let your belly and shoulders soften with each exhale. Feel your back ribs expand as you inhale. Stay for 30 seconds to several minutes.

Easy Modifications

  • Tight Knees or Ankles — place a rolled blanket or folded towel behind your knees or under your ankles.
  • Hips Don't Reach Heels — put a firm pillow, folded blanket or bolster between your thighs and calves so your hips are supported, not hanging.
  • Neck or Forehead Strain — rest your forehead on a folded towel, stacked hands or cushion so your neck is completely relaxed.
  • Wrist or Shoulder Discomfort — bring your arms back by your sides with soft elbows, instead of reaching far forward.

When to Skip or Adjust

This pose is gentle for most beginners, but skip it or choose a supported reclined or seated alternative if you have acute knee or ankle pain, significant lower back disc issues that worsen with forward folding, dizziness, or you are pregnant and the wide-knee variation feels uncomfortable on your abdomen.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should I stay in Child's Pose? Most people hold it for 30 seconds to 2 minutes as a reset between poses, or 3–5 minutes as a quiet relaxation stretch at the end of a session or before the [LINK: Article #3].

Is Child's Pose good for lower back pain? Yes, for many people with mild lower back stiffness, it gently lengthens the back muscles without strain — provided you do not force your hips back into sharp pain.

Should I keep my knees wide or together? Wide knees open your hips more and give your torso extra space to settle. Knees closer together create a slightly more focused stretch along the back of your torso. Choose whichever feels more natural today.
