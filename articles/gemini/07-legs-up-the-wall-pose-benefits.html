If you could only practise one restorative pose at the end of a long day, Legs-Up-the-Wall would be hard to beat. Unlike active poses that require strength, balance or deep flexibility, this gentle inversion lets gravity and the wall do almost all the work. Just 5–10 minutes resting with your legs elevated against a wall can ease heaviness in your feet, calm a busy nervous system, and prepare your body for deeper rest — an effortless daily habit. It is also featured as a calming finisher in the [LINK: Article #3].

What Is It?

Sanskrit: Viparita means "inverted" or "reversed", Karani means "in action". Resting on your back, flat on the floor, with your legs vertically against the wall gently reverses the downward pull of gravity your lower body experiences while standing and sitting. Your spine, head and legs are fully supported, so your muscles can let go without effort.

Key Benefits Daily

  • Relieves Tired, Heavy Legs and Feet — elevating your legs encourages fluid and venous circulation back toward your upper body, easing mild swelling and fatigue after standing or walking for hours.
  • Calms Your Nervous System & Eases Stress — lying still with your legs supported activates the parasympathetic ("rest-and-digest") response, lowering tension.
  • Supports Better Sleep Quality — a few minutes before bed signals your body to slow down, especially paired with slow breathing (see the [LINK: Article #5]).
  • Gently Releases Lower Back & Hamstring Tension — a mild passive stretch for the backs of your legs while your lower back settles into the floor, without weight on your spine.

How to Do It Step by Step

Find a clear space next to a wall, with a mat, folded blanket or firm cushion perpendicular to the wall.

Sit Sideways Near the Wall

Sit with your right hip touching or a few inches from the base of the wall, knees bent.

Swing Your Legs Up Gently

Lean back onto your forearms and shoulders while swinging both legs upward so the backs of your legs rest vertically against the wall.

Adjust Your Distance

Lie flat, shoulders relaxed, head on the floor or a thin pillow. If your hamstrings feel tight or your lower back lifts off the floor, scoot your hips 4–8 inches away from the wall until your legs feel relaxed.

Rest Your Arms and Breathe

Open your arms by your sides, palms up, or rest one hand on your belly and one on your chest. Close your eyes and breathe slowly and naturally.

Hold 5–10 Minutes

Stay still and comfortable. To come out safely, bend your knees to your chest, roll gently onto one side, pause for 2–3 breaths, then press up to sitting.

Easy Modifications

  • Tight Hamstrings — move your hips further from the wall and allow a soft bend in your knees. Your legs do not need to be locked straight.
  • Lower Back Support — place a folded towel or thin pillow under your hips or pelvis for gentle elevation, or skip it if lying flat feels better. For more spinal comfort, see the [LINK: Article #4].
  • No Wall Space — lie on the floor and rest your calves on the seat of a sturdy chair or sofa for a similar restorative effect.

Who Should Avoid or Modify?

This pose is gentle for most healthy adults, but consult a healthcare professional before practising inversions if you have uncontrolled high blood pressure, glaucoma or serious eye conditions, severe lower back or neck injuries, or you are in the later stages of pregnancy. Stop immediately if you feel tingling, numbness or pain in your legs or lower back.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should I hold it each day? 5 minutes is plenty for beginners. Once you are comfortable, 8–12 minutes in the evening or after long periods of standing is ideal.

Should my legs be completely straight? No — a slight natural bend, or moving your hips a few inches back, keeps your hamstrings relaxed without strain.

What is the best time of day? Late afternoon, after work, or 15–20 minutes before bedtime is ideal for unwinding. For an energising start to the day, try the 10-minute morning yoga routine.
