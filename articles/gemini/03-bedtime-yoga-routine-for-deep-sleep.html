Lying in bed with a tired body but a racing mind is common. Stress, screen time, and physical tension keep your nervous system in alert "fight-or-flight" mode, making it hard to fall asleep and stay asleep. Gentle bedtime yoga is a natural bridge between a busy day and restful sleep. Unlike energising daytime flows, evening yoga is slow, supported stretches and long, calming breaths that signal your body it is safe to let go. Practise this 10-minute sequence on your mat or directly on your bed, 20–30 minutes before sleep. If neck and shoulder tension from your computer is part of the problem, try the [LINK: Article #2] earlier in the evening.

How Evening Yoga Improves Sleep

  • Activates Relaxation Response — slow breathing stimulates the parasympathetic nervous system, lowering heart rate and cortisol.
  • Releases Physical Restlessness — gentle stretches ease your hips, lower back and legs so you can lie comfortably.
  • Quiets Mental Chatter — focusing on slow, rhythmic breaths gives your busy mind a calm anchor.

The 10-minute bedtime yoga sequence

Dim the lights, put your phone on silent, and move without rushing. Hold each pose 60–90 seconds and breathe slowly through your nose.

1. Seated Easy Forward Fold — Sukhasana Variation — 1.5 minutes

Sit cross-legged with your hands in front of you. Inhale and lengthen your spine. Exhale and walk your hands forward as your torso folds over your legs, forearms resting on the floor or a pillow, head and neck relaxed.

Benefit: inward calm, releasing your upper back and outer hips.

2. Reclined Butterfly Pose — Supta Baddha Konasana — 2 minutes

Lie on your back, knees bent, soles of your feet together, knees falling open. Place a pillow or folded blanket under each knee. Rest one hand on your belly and one on your chest, and feel your breath rise and fall.

Benefit: opens tight hips gently and encourages deep diaphragmatic breathing.

3. Wind-Relieving Pose — Pawanmuktasana — 1.5 minutes

Lying on your back, draw both knees to your chest and wrap your arms around your shins. Keep your head, shoulders and tailbone relaxed, and rock gently from side to side to massage your lower back.

Benefit: relieves lower back stiffness and releases trapped tension in your abdomen.

4. Supine Spinal Twist — Supta Matsyendrasana — 2 minutes (1 minute per side)

Hug your right knee in and keep your left leg straight. Use your left hand to guide your right knee across to the left, and stretch your right arm out in a "T" or cactus shape. Keep both shoulders heavy on the floor and gaze to the ceiling or your right hand. Switch sides.

Benefit: decompresses your spine and melts residual tension from sitting or standing.

5. Legs-Up-the-Wall Pose — Viparita Karani — 2 minutes

Bring your hips close to the wall or headboard and stretch your legs straight up against the wall. Rest your arms by your sides, palms up, and close your eyes. If your hamstrings feel tight, move your hips a few inches away from the wall.

Benefit: the most restorative pose for reducing fatigue, calming anxiety, and preparing for deep rest.

6. Corpse Pose with 4-7-8 Breathing — Savasana — 1 minute

Lower your legs and stretch out flat, or slip under a blanket. Let your feet fall open and relax your jaw, forehead and shoulders. Breathe for 4 rounds: inhale through your nose for 4 seconds, hold softly for 7 seconds, exhale through your mouth or nose for 8 seconds.

Benefit: slows your nervous system and transitions your mind into sleep.

Simple Habits for Better Sleep

  • Keep Screens Away Afterward — avoid messages and scrolling so blue light does not reset your alertness.
  • Use Props Freely — pillows, cushions and folded blankets make evening yoga effortless and restorative.
  • Start Morning Right Too — pair evening relaxation with the 10-minute morning yoga routine.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I do bedtime yoga directly on my bed? Yes — gentle seated and reclined poses like the Supine Twist, Reclined Butterfly and Savasana work wonderfully on a firm mattress, and you can drift off to sleep easily afterward.

How long before bed should I practise? 15–30 minutes before your planned sleep time is ideal.
