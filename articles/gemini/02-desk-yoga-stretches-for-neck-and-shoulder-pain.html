Sitting at a computer or phone for hours strains your upper body. Rounded shoulders and a forward head create tightness, tension headaches, and neck and upper-back discomfort. You do not need to change your clothes or roll out a mat. These 7 stretches can be done from your office chair in under 7 minutes. For a fuller start to the day, pair them with the 10-minute morning yoga routine.

The 7 desk yoga stretches

1. Seated Neck Release (Side Stretch)

Sit tall near the front edge of your chair, feet flat on the floor. Drop your right ear to your right shoulder. Rest your right hand lightly on the left side of your head without pulling, and let your left fingertips reach toward the floor. Hold for 5 slow breaths on each side.

Targets: the upper trapezius.

2. Chin-to-Chest Stretch (Back of Neck)

Keep your spine straight and your shoulders relaxed. Lower your chin to your chest until you feel a comfortable stretch along the back of your neck, keeping your chest lifted. Hold for 5–6 slow breaths.

3. Shoulder Rolls & Shrug Release

Inhale and lift your shoulders to your ears. Exhale with a sigh, roll them back and drop them down. Do 6 times backward and 4 times forward.

Benefit: releases upper-back tightness and increases blood flow to the shoulder joints.

4. Seated Eagle Arms (Garudasana Arms)

Stretch your arms straight in front of you at shoulder height. Cross your right arm under your left, bend your elbows, and lift your forearms up so the backs or palms of your hands press together. Lift your elbows slightly and press your forearms away from your face. Hold for 5 slow breaths, then switch arms.

Benefit: best for opening the area between your shoulder blades.

5. Seated Cat-Cow Stretch

Rest your hands on your knees or thighs. Inhale: arch your back, draw your shoulder blades together, and lift your chest and gaze (Cow). Exhale: round your spine, tuck your chin, and draw your belly in (Cat). Flow for 6–8 rounds with your breath.

6. Seated Spinal Twist

Sit tall, feet hip-width apart. Inhale and lengthen your spine. Exhale and twist to the right, placing your left hand outside your right thigh and your right hand on the back or seat of your chair. Look softly over your right shoulder without forcing your neck. Hold for 5 breaths on the right, then repeat on the left.

Benefit: decompresses your spine and relieves upper and lower back stiffness.

7. Chest Opener Behind the Chair

Sit toward the front of your chair and reach your hands behind you to hold the sides or backrest (or interlace your fingers behind your lower back). Roll your shoulders back, squeeze your shoulder blades together, and lift your sternum. Hold for 5 deep breaths as your chest and front shoulders expand.

Benefit: counteracts hunching and tight pectorals.

Quick Tips

  • 60-Minute Rule — set a silent timer every hour to stand up, take 3 deep breaths, or do 2 stretches.
  • Check Screen Height — the top of your monitor should be at or slightly below eye level.
  • Breathe Slowly — holding your breath keeps your muscles tense; slow exhales signal your nervous system to relax.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I do desk yoga? A quick 5-minute session once in the morning and once in the afternoon is ideal. Even 60 seconds every hour makes a noticeable difference.

Can desk yoga prevent tension headaches? Yes — many workday headaches are triggered by tight neck and shoulder muscles. Releasing that tension reduces strain at the base of the skull.
