Lower back pain is one of the most common reasons people turn to yoga. Discomfort from desk sitting, tight hip flexors, poor posture and everyday stress can all build up — and gentle movement can bring significant relief. But when your lower back is already tender, aggressive stretching and deep forward folds can worsen stiffness. The key is slow, supported poses that decompress your spine, strengthen core stability, and release tight hip and glute muscles. These 6 safe beginner poses take about 10 minutes at home. Before You Start: 3 Safety Rules

  • Never Push Through Sharp Pain — a mild, comforting stretch is good; sharp, pinching or radiating pain means back off.
  • Bend Your Knees Freely — tight hamstrings pull on your lower back, and a soft bend protects your lumbar spine.
  • Move With Your Breath — holding your breath creates muscle guarding; slow exhales help your deep spinal muscles release.

The 6 safe poses

Use a mat or firm carpet. Hold each pose for 5–8 slow breaths, about 45–60 seconds.

1. Pelvic Tilts (Supine Spinal Warm-Up)

Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat and hip-width apart, arms by your sides. Inhale and let the small natural curve form under your lower back. Exhale and press your lower back flat into the mat, engaging your lower belly. Repeat 8–10 times.

Benefit: gently wakes the deep core muscles that stabilise your lower back.

2. Cat-Cow Pose — Marjaryasana-Bitilasana

Come to tabletop. Inhale: drop your belly and lift your chest (Cow). Exhale: press your hands, tuck your tailbone, and round your upper back (Cat). Keep the movement smooth, not an extreme arch. Flow for 6–8 breaths.

Benefit: improves circulation between your spinal discs and mobilises a stiff back.

3. Child's Pose — Balasana

From tabletop, bring your big toes together, knees wide, and hips back to your heels. Walk your hands forward, rest your forehead on the mat, a folded towel or a pillow, and breathe into the back of your ribcage. Tip: place a rolled blanket between your calves and thighs if your hips do not reach your heels.

Benefit: softly lengthens the muscles along your spine.

4. Knees-to-Chest Pose — Apanasana

Lie on your back, draw both knees to your chest, and hold behind your thighs or across your shins. Keep your head, neck and tailbone heavy on the floor, and rock gently from side to side for a lower-back massage. Hold for 6–8 slow breaths.

Benefit: stretches the erector spinae and relieves lumbar compression.

5. Supine Figure-Four Stretch (Reclined Pigeon)

Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat. Cross your right ankle over your left thigh just below the knee, making a triangle shape, and flex your right foot. For a deeper stretch, lift your left foot, interlace your hands behind your left thigh, and draw your legs toward your chest. Hold for 5–6 breaths per side.

Benefit: releases tight glutes and piriformis muscles that mimic or worsen lower back pain, while your spine stays supported.

6. Supported Bridge Pose — Setu Bandhasana

Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat and hip-width apart, heels a few inches from your glutes. Press your feet and arms down, inhale and lift your hips slightly — only as high as is comfortable without pinching your lower back. Keep your knees aligned with your hips. Hold for 4–5 breaths, then roll your spine down one vertebra at a time. You can slide a firm pillow or yoga block under your sacrum for a restorative version.

Benefit: strengthens your posterior chain and opens tight hip flexors.

When to See a Doctor

Consult a healthcare professional before practising if you have numbness or tingling down your legs (sciatica), sudden severe pain after a fall or injury, or pain that worsens with light movement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it okay to do yoga every day for lower back pain? Yes — gentle restorative poses like Pelvic Tilts, Cat-Cow and the Supine Figure-Four can be practised daily. Pairing them with the 10-minute morning yoga routine helps prevent stiffness.

Which poses should I avoid with lower back pain? Avoid deep standing forward bends with straight legs, intense backbends (Full Wheel), and heavy spinal twists until your back is stronger and pain-free.
