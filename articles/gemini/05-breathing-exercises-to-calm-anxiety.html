When anxiety hits — before an important meeting, on a stressful afternoon, or late at night — your breathing changes first. Shallow, rapid breaths high in your chest signal your brain that you are in danger and keep stress hormones flowing. The fastest way to interrupt that stress loop is intentional breathing. In yoga traditions, mindful breathing is called pranayama (breath regulation). No mat, extra space or 30 minutes are needed — just 5 quiet minutes can steady your nervous system. Why Slow Breathing Calms Anxiety So Quickly

  • Activates the Vagus Nerve — longer, unhurried exhales stimulate the vagus nerve and shift your body from "fight-or-flight" to "rest-and-digest".
  • Slows Heart Rate — rhythmic breathing brings a racing heart and tight chest back toward a calm baseline.
  • Anchors Attention — counting your breaths and following the air gives anxious thoughts less room to spiral.

The 5 breathing exercises

Sit in a chair with your feet flat, or cross-legged on a mat or bed. Keep your spine tall yet relaxed, your shoulders away from your ears, and your jaw soft.

1. Box Breathing — Sama Vritti — 1 minute

Use equal counts for inhale, hold, exhale and pause, a steady balanced rhythm for high-pressure moments. Inhale through your nose for 4 seconds, hold softly for 4 seconds (no strain), exhale through your nose or mouth for 4 seconds, then pause with your lungs empty for 4 seconds. Repeat the 4-part cycle for 1 minute (about 3–4 rounds).

2. Extended Exhale Breathing — 1 minute

Exhaling longer than you inhale signals calm. Inhale through your nose for 4 seconds, then exhale slowly and smoothly for 6–8 seconds, letting your shoulders drop. Continue for 1 minute, smooth and unforced.

3. Alternate Nostril Breathing — Nadi Shodhana — 1.5 minutes

A classic pranayama to balance mental energy and quiet overthinking. Rest your left hand on your knee. Bring your right hand near your face, with your index and middle fingers lightly between your eyebrows (or folded inward), and your thumb and ring finger ready. Close your right nostril with your right thumb and inhale through your left nostril for 4 seconds. Close your left nostril with your ring finger, release your thumb, and exhale through your right nostril for 4–6 seconds. Inhale through your right nostril for 4 seconds, switch, and exhale through your left for 4–6 seconds. Complete 5–6 full rounds (about 1.5 minutes).

4. Belly (Diaphragmatic) Breathing — 1 minute

Shallow chest breathing goes with nervousness. Belly breathing encourages deeper, fuller oxygen exchange. Place one hand on your abdomen below your navel and the other on your chest. Inhale through your nose so your belly rises gently while your chest stays relatively quiet. Exhale slowly as your belly softens inward. Continue for 1 minute, smooth and natural.

5. Humming Bee Breath — Bhramari Pranayama — 30 seconds to 1 minute

The gentle vibration from humming on your exhale soothes your mind and face and jaw tension. Inhale through your nose for 4 seconds. Exhale slowly with your lips gently closed, making a soft, steady humming "mmmm" sound until your breath finishes. Repeat 4–6 times.

Tips for Practising Safely

  • Never Force or Strain — if holding your breath makes you lightheaded, shorten or drop the hold and focus on slow breathing.
  • Keep It Gentle — it should feel soothing, not like a contest for lung capacity.
  • Build a Daily Anchor — practise 5 minutes once or twice a day, alongside the 10-minute morning yoga routine, to build long-term calm.

Frequently Asked Questions

How fast can breathing calm anxiety? Many people notice a subtle drop in physical tension, jaw clenching or rapid breathing within 2–5 minutes of slow, rhythmic breathing.

Are pranayama exercises safe for beginners? Yes, gentle breathwork with comfortable counts is widely suitable. If you have asthma or respiratory issues, or feel dizzy during breath holds, keep your counts short and breathe naturally without holding.
