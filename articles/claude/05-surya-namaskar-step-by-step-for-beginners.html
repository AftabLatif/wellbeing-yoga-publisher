If you could only learn one yoga sequence as a beginner, Surya Namaskar would be it.

Surya Namaskar — also known as Sun Salutation — is a series of 12 yoga poses performed in a flowing sequence. It works your entire body, combines strength and flexibility, synchronizes movement with breath, and can be completed in just a few minutes.

It is practiced by millions of people worldwide, every single morning, as the foundation of their yoga practice.

In this guide, you will learn exactly how to do Surya Namaskar step by step, with clear instructions for each pose — even if you are a complete beginner.

What is Surya Namaskar?

Surya Namaskar is a Sanskrit term. Surya means Sun and Namaskar means salutation or greeting. So it literally means "greeting the sun."

Traditionally, it is practiced at sunrise, facing the direction of the rising sun. However, it can be practiced at any time of day on an empty stomach.

One complete round of Surya Namaskar consists of 12 poses performed twice — once leading with the right leg and once with the left leg. This equals one full cycle.

Benefits of Surya Namaskar

Before we get into the steps, here is why Surya Namaskar is so powerful:

  • Full body workout — works every major muscle group
  • Burns calories — 1 round burns approximately 13–14 calories
  • Improves flexibility — stretches and strengthens simultaneously
  • Boosts metabolism — particularly effective when done in the morning
  • Improves posture — strengthens the spine and core
  • Reduces stress — the breathing synchronization calms the nervous system
  • Improves blood circulation — the alternating forward and backward bends pump blood throughout the body
  • Requires no equipment — just a mat and your body

Before You Begin

Important tips for beginners:

  • Practice on an empty stomach — wait at least 3–4 hours after a meal
  • Use a non-slip yoga mat
  • Wear comfortable, stretchy clothing
  • Start slowly — focus on getting the form right before increasing speed
  • Breathe through your nose throughout the practice
  • Never force a pose — work within your comfortable range of motion

Surya Namaskar — 12 Steps for Beginners

Step 1 — Pranamasana (Prayer Pose)

How to do it: Stand at the front of your mat with your feet together. Bring your palms together in front of your chest in a prayer position. Stand tall, relax your shoulders, and close your eyes for a moment.

Breathing: Breathe normally. Take a deep breath in and out before beginning.

What it does: Calms the mind, centers your focus, and prepares your body for the sequence.

Step 2 — Hastauttanasana (Raised Arms Pose)

How to do it: Inhale deeply and raise both arms up and slightly back over your head. Arch your back gently, lifting your chest toward the ceiling. Keep your arms shoulder-width apart. Look up slightly.

Breathing: Inhale as you raise your arms.

What it does: Stretches the entire front of your body — abdomen, chest, and shoulders. Opens the lungs for deeper breathing.

Step 3 — Hastapadasana (Standing Forward Bend)

How to do it: Exhale and bend forward from your hips (not your waist). Bring your hands down toward the floor on either side of your feet. If you cannot reach the floor, bend your knees slightly — that is completely fine for beginners.

Breathing: Exhale as you fold forward.

What it does: Stretches the hamstrings, calves, and spine. Increases blood flow to the brain.

Step 4 — Ashwa Sanchalanasana (Low Lunge Pose)

How to do it: Inhale and step your right leg back as far as possible. Lower your right knee to the floor. Your left knee should be directly above your left ankle (at a 90-degree angle). Look forward and lift your chest.

Breathing: Inhale as you step back.

What it does: Stretches the hip flexors and groin. Strengthens the legs and improves balance.

Step 5 — Dandasana (Plank Pose)

How to do it: Hold your breath and step your left leg back to meet the right. Your body should now form a straight line from your head to your heels — like the top of a push-up position. Keep your core engaged and your hips level (do not let them sag or lift too high).

Breathing: Hold the breath briefly as you move into this pose.

What it does: Builds core strength, arm strength, and full-body stability.

Step 6 — Ashtanga Namaskara (Eight-Limbed Pose)

How to do it: Exhale and gently lower your knees to the floor first. Then lower your chest and chin to the floor, keeping your hips slightly lifted. Your eight points of contact with the floor are: chin, chest, two hands, two knees, and two feet.

Breathing: Exhale as you lower down.

What it does: Strengthens the arms, chest, and back. This is the yoga equivalent of a push-up modification.

Step 7 — Bhujangasana (Cobra Pose)

How to do it: Inhale and slide your body forward, pressing your palms into the floor. Lift your chest up and forward into a gentle backbend. Keep your elbows slightly bent (do not lock them straight). Your legs and hips remain on the floor. Look forward or slightly upward.

Breathing: Inhale as you lift into cobra.

What it does: Strengthens the back muscles, opens the chest, and improves spinal flexibility.

Step 8 — Adho Mukha Svanasana (Downward Facing Dog)

How to do it: Exhale and lift your hips up and back, straightening your arms and legs to form an inverted V-shape with your body. Press your palms firmly into the floor. Try to press your heels toward the floor (they do not need to touch — especially for beginners). Relax your head and neck.

Breathing: Exhale as you lift into downward dog. Take 3–5 breaths here.

What it does: Stretches the entire back of the body — hamstrings, calves, spine, and shoulders. One of the most beneficial poses in all of yoga.

Step 9 — Ashwa Sanchalanasana (Low Lunge — Other Side)

How to do it: Inhale and step your right foot forward between your hands. Lower your left knee to the floor. This mirrors Step 4 but on the opposite side. Look forward and lift your chest.

Breathing: Inhale as you step forward.

What it does: Stretches the hip flexors on the other side of the body.

Step 10 — Hastapadasana (Standing Forward Bend)

How to do it: Exhale and bring your left foot forward to meet the right. You are back in the forward bend position from Step 3. Let your upper body hang heavy, hands reaching toward the floor.

Breathing: Exhale as you fold forward.

What it does: Re-stretches the hamstrings and calves. Allows blood to flow back toward the upper body.

Step 11 — Hastauttanasana (Raised Arms Pose)

How to do it: Inhale and sweep your arms up and back overhead, returning to the raised arms position from Step 2. Arch gently backward, lifting your chest.

Breathing: Inhale as you rise and reach up.

What it does: Stretches the front of the body and opens the chest after the forward bending sequence.

Step 12 — Pranamasana (Prayer Pose)

How to do it: Exhale and bring your palms back together in front of your chest. Stand tall. This completes one half-round of Surya Namaskar.

Breathing: Exhale as you return to center.

What it does: Returns your body to a neutral, centered position and completes the cycle.

Now repeat Steps 1–12, this time stepping the LEFT leg back in Step 4 and forward in Step 9. This completes one full round of Surya Namaskar.

How Many Rounds Should a Beginner Do?

Week 1–2: 2–4 rounds per day

Week 3–4: 5–8 rounds per day

Month 2 onwards: 10–12 rounds per day

At a moderate pace, 12 rounds takes approximately 15–20 minutes and burns around 150–180 calories.

Common Beginner Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1 — Rushing through the poses

Surya Namaskar is not a race. Each pose should be held briefly and performed with awareness. Rushing reduces the benefits significantly.

Mistake 2 — Forgetting to breathe

The breath synchronization is what makes Surya Namaskar so powerful. Never hold your breath (except in the brief transition at plank pose).

Mistake 3 — Forcing flexibility

If you cannot touch the floor in the forward bend, bend your knees. If Downward Dog is too intense, keep your knees slightly bent. Work within your range.

Mistake 4 — Skipping the warm-up

If you are practicing in the morning when your body is stiff, do 2–3 minutes of gentle movement before beginning your first round.

Final Thoughts

Surya Namaskar is one of the most complete exercises ever designed. It stretches, strengthens, and energizes your entire body in just a few minutes — making it the perfect practice for busy beginners.

Start with 4 rounds per day. Focus on getting the breathing and form right. Increase gradually as your body adapts.

Within 3–4 weeks of daily practice, you will notice improved flexibility, better posture, more energy throughout the day, and a calmer, clearer mind.

This one sequence, done consistently every morning, can genuinely transform your health.
